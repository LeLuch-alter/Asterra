You are working on a student project called **Asterra**.

Asterra is an online scientific collaboration platform inspired by the idea of “GitHub for Science”.

The goal is not to create another simple website with profiles and project cards. Asterra should feel like a living environment where scientific research has its own structure, history, connections and evolution.

The platform allows students, researchers and mentors to discover scientific projects, join research teams, create their own projects and work together on research.

The existing core functionality of Asterra includes:

- User profiles
- Researcher profiles
- Scientific project discovery
- Project creation and editing
- Project members and collaboration
- Research results
- Search and filtering
- Mentor/researcher discovery
- Science news
- AI Match
- AI Research Assistant
- AI Research Roadmap

The new central concept of Asterra is:

# RESEARCH AS A LIVING GRAPH

A scientific project should not be represented as one static page containing text, members and files.

Instead, every research project should behave like a connected research ecosystem.

A project can contain and connect:

- Research question
- Hypothesis
- Methodology
- Research roadmap
- Experiments
- Research results
- Researchers
- Mentors
- Scientific sources
- Related projects
- Different versions of ideas
- Research branches

These elements should be visually and conceptually connected.

For example:

Project:
“AI-based Water Pollution Detection”

can contain:

Research Question
↓
Hypothesis
↓
Methodology
↓
Experiment #1
↓
Result #1

At the same time:

Researchers
↓
Project

Scientific Sources
↓
Methodology

AI Research Roadmap
↓
Research stages

Related Projects
↓
Project

The user should feel that they are exploring a research network rather than simply opening a database record.

---

# RESEARCH GRAPH

The Research Graph is the main distinctive feature of Asterra.

The graph represents the relationships between different parts of research.

A user should be able to visually understand:

- what the research is trying to answer;
- what hypothesis is being tested;
- how the research is being conducted;
- which experiments were performed;
- which researchers contributed;
- which results came from which experiments;
- which scientific sources support different parts of the research;
- which projects are related;
- how the research evolved over time.

The graph should not feel like a technical diagram.

It should feel like an interactive map of a living research project.

Clicking a node should reveal the corresponding research content.

For example:

Clicking an experiment should show:

- experiment purpose;
- author;
- methodology;
- input/data;
- result;
- related research content.

Clicking a researcher should show their contribution to the project.

Clicking a scientific source should show which part of the research it is connected to.

---

# RESEARCH TIMELINE

Every research project should also have a timeline showing how the research evolved.

Example:

September 10
Project created

September 12
Anna joined the research

September 14
Research question changed

September 16
Hypothesis updated

September 18
Experiment #1 added

September 20
First result published

September 21
Research roadmap generated

The timeline should make the project feel alive.

It should show that research is not created once and forgotten.

Research develops over time.

---

# RESEARCH VERSIONS

Asterra should preserve the evolution of important research elements.

For example:

Research Question v1:
“How does pollution affect water?”

Research Question v2:
“How does microplastic concentration affect freshwater quality?”

Research Question v3:
“How does microplastic concentration affect freshwater microorganisms?”

Users should be able to understand how and why an idea changed.

The goal is to bring the concept of version history from software development into scientific research.

Asterra should communicate the idea:

“Research evolves just like software.”

---

# RESEARCH FORK

One of the most important features inspired by GitHub is:

**Fork Research**

A user should be able to create a new research direction based on an existing project.

Example:

Original research:

“AI-based Forest Fire Detection”

Another researcher decides to build on the idea.

They create:

“AI-based Forest Fire Detection — Satellite Dataset”

The new project can inherit the original research idea while developing:

- a different hypothesis;
- a different dataset;
- a different methodology;
- different experiments;
- different results.

The original project remains unchanged.

The new project becomes a research branch.

This creates a relationship between the projects:

Original Research
↓
Fork
↓
New Research Direction

Asterra should visualize these relationships so that users can discover how scientific ideas branch and evolve.

This is one of the strongest connections between Asterra and the “GitHub for Science” concept.

---

# SCIENTIFIC SOURCES

Scientific sources should not simply exist as a list at the bottom of a project.

They should be connected to the research itself.

For example:

A scientific source can be connected to:

- the research question;
- hypothesis;
- methodology;
- experiment;
- result.

This allows users to understand why a source is relevant.

Instead of:

“References: 8 papers”

Asterra should encourage:

“This paper supports this part of the research.”

---

# AI INTEGRATION

Asterra has three main AI features.

Do not introduce additional major AI products unless explicitly requested.

## AI MATCH

AI Match helps researchers find suitable collaborators and mentors.

The system looks at:

- research field;
- skills;
- interests;
- experience;
- project needs;
- previous projects.

The important part is not only recommending a person.

Asterra should explain why the person is relevant.

For example:

“Strong overlap in machine learning and environmental research.”

The recommendation should feel understandable rather than like a mysterious AI score.

---

## AI RESEARCH ASSISTANT

The AI Research Assistant exists inside the research environment.

It should understand the context of the current project.

Users should be able to ask it to:

- summarize research;
- explain a research section;
- identify unclear parts;
- suggest improvements;
- suggest research questions;
- review research structure.

The assistant should feel like a research companion that understands the current project rather than a generic chatbot.

---

## AI RESEARCH ROADMAP

The AI Research Roadmap turns a research idea into a possible sequence of research stages.

For example:

Idea
↓
Research Question
↓
Hypothesis
↓
Literature Review
↓
Methodology
↓
Data Collection
↓
Experiment
↓
Analysis
↓
Results
↓
Conclusion

The generated roadmap should become part of the Research Graph.

Users should be able to modify the roadmap because AI should provide a starting point, not dictate the research.

---

# DISCOVERY

Asterra should make scientific discovery itself part of the experience.

Users should be able to discover:

- research projects;
- researchers;
- mentors;
- scientific opportunities;
- related research;
- science news.

A project should lead naturally to other projects.

A researcher should lead to projects they contributed to.

A result should lead to related research.

A scientific source should lead to projects that use it.

This creates a network rather than isolated pages.

---

# OVERALL PRODUCT IDEA

Asterra should communicate three major ideas:

### 1. Research is collaborative

Scientists should be able to find people, mentors and collaborators.

### 2. Research is connected

Ideas, experiments, results, people and sources should be connected.

### 3. Research evolves

Research questions change.
Hypotheses change.
Experiments create results.
Projects branch into new directions.

Asterra should make this evolution visible.

---

# THE FEELING OF THE PRODUCT

Asterra should feel like:

- GitHub for scientific research;
- a research network;
- a living map of scientific ideas;
- a workspace for collaborative research.

Avoid making it feel like:

- a normal university portal;
- a generic social network;
- a simple academic blog;
- a normal project management tool.

The distinctive identity of Asterra should come from the idea that:

**Every research project is a living system of ideas, people, evidence, experiments and results.**

The user should be able to enter a project and visually understand:

“What is this research trying to discover?”

“Who is working on it?”

“What has already been done?”

“What evidence supports it?”

“What happened over time?”

“What research could come next?”

And most importantly:

**“Where can this idea go next?”**

That is the core product philosophy of Asterra.
